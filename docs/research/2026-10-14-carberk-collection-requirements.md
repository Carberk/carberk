# Carberk collection requirements evidence, 2026-10-14

Research checkpoint: 2026-10-14. Scope: first-party longitudinal pseudonymous product analytics, separately evaluated operational diagnostics, and the nine accepted audience regions. This is shaping evidence for #605 and #608, not an authorization to collect, publish a policy, submit a store form, buy infrastructure or certify compliance. It preserves the accepted regional fallback in #605.

## Decision needed before collection

Preserve broad useful default-on collection wherever its basis and store rules support it. The founder prefers explaining collection in Terms and rejects unnecessary optional product settings. This preference does not settle required consent, withdrawal or deletion. No blanket analytics toggle is proposed for every region or all diagnostics. The first unresolved gate is the Ashgabat pilot itself. The official TM statute contains consent, domestic storage and cross-border requirements that need a responsible legal reviewer to apply to the actual operator, identifiers, guest timeline and hosting design. Overseas discovery can run later by phase. It does not require nine deployments now.

Pseudonymous guest installation IDs, account analytics IDs and restricted timelines must be evaluated as potentially identifiable data. Keeping names out and grouping fewer than five actors are safeguards, not proof of anonymization. The accepted 90-day product, 30-day diagnostic and 24-month aggregate periods are maximum product-policy limits, subject to shorter purpose/legal limits. Do not expand them to solve a legal requirement.

## Verified official facts and remaining applicability questions

### Turkmenistan, initial pilot

The parliament's published Law on Information about Private Life and its Protection defines personal information broadly in Article 1. Article 7 sets written consent as the default, subject to Article 9 exceptions. Article 8 permits withdrawal and electronic or other protected consent methods. Article 12(2) specifies storage in a database located in TM. Article 17 ties foreign transfer to a TM database and destination protection, with stated exceptions. Article 18 requires anonymization for statistical, sociological or scientific research. Article 7(9) also limits contracting out collection/processing. The Russian page identifies itself as a translation of the state-language law. [Official parliamentary statute, Articles 1, 7–9, 12, 17–19](https://mejlis.gov.tm/single-law/66?lang=ru), [state-language version](https://mejlis.gov.tm/single-law/66?lang=tm).

Review needed: lawful basis and valid electronic consent format for guest/account analytics; meaning of statistical anonymization for longitudinal timelines; domestic storage and processor/foreign access rules for Railway reviewer data, TM production, backups and GlitchTip. Do not collect identity-document data just to invent a consent mechanism. Obtain an approved interpretation and minimal lawful flow first.

### China, later phase

PIPL Article 3 covers overseas processing to offer services to, or analyze behavior of, people in China. Articles 13–17 set bases, informed consent and withdrawal rules. Article 39 requires destination-recipient information and separate consent for foreign provision. Article 47 covers deletion, including withdrawal and expired purpose. [Official PIPL, first part](https://en.spp.gov.cn/2021-12/29/c_948419.htm), [foreign-transfer and rights provisions](https://en.spp.gov.cn/2021-12/29/c_948419_2.htm).

CAC's 2024 cross-border rules exempt some small non-sensitive transfers from assessment/standard-contract/certification mechanisms. Article 10 preserves notification, separate-consent and impact-assessment obligations. A size exemption does not establish consent-free analytics. [Official CAC rules, Articles 5, 7, 8, 10](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2024-03/22/c_1712776611775634.htm).

Review needed: exact cross-border applicability to a foreign operator collecting directly, PIPL basis for optional behavioral measurement, local representation, impact assessment, current 2025–26 implementing rules and any sensitive/important-data classification. TM citizenship does not remove rules for people in China.

### Russia, later phase

An official ministry's consolidated 152-FZ text includes the February and June 2025 amendments. Article 1(1.1) extends to specified foreign processing of Russian citizens' data. Article 18(5), amended by 23-FZ, prohibits listed collection-stage operations on Russian citizens' personal data using databases outside Russia, subject to exceptions. Article 9 requires consent separately from other accepted documents. Article 12 requires a separate advance cross-border notification. Articles 6, 9 and 21 address bases, withdrawal and termination/deletion. [Official ministry law text](https://mintrud.gov.ru/docs/laws/130).

Review needed: who and which processing the citizenship-specific requirements cover, collection versus subsequent transfer topology, notification timing and any later amendments. This ministry consolidation ends at June 2025 and is not proof of a complete October 2026 consolidation. Do not apply a Russian-citizen rule to all Turkmen people in Russia without analysis, or infer an exemption for them from citizenship alone.

### Turkey, later phase

KVKK Article 5 permits explicit consent and specified alternatives, including necessary legitimate interests subject to rights. Article 7 requires erasure, destruction or anonymization once grounds end. Articles 10–11 cover notice and rights. The amended Article 9 provides foreign-transfer routes through adequacy or safeguards; exceptional occasional-transfer routes are separate. [Current official English law](https://www.kvkk.gov.tr/Icerik/6649/Personal-Data-Protection-Law), [official 2024 transfer regulations and standard contracts](https://www.kvkk.gov.tr/Icerik/7999/Announcement-on-the-Translation-of-By-Law-on-The-Procedures-And-Principles-For-The-Transfer-of-Personal-Data-Abroad-and-Standard-Contract-Texts).

Review needed: a documented basis assessment for longitudinal analytics, separate notice/consent if relied on, applicable controller registration/representation, and a valid recurring transfer route. Do not treat recurring telemetry as an occasional consent exception.

### UAE/Dubai, later phase

Federal Decree-Law 45/2021 Article 4 establishes consent with specified exceptions. Articles 5–6 cover minimization, retention and provable consent/withdrawal. Articles 15–17 cover correction, erasure and stopping processing; Articles 22–23 govern foreign transfers. Article 2 includes scope and exclusions, including free zones with their own data-protection legislation. [Official UAE legislation PDF](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1972/download).

Review needed: operator establishment and whether federal, DIFC or other excluded regimes apply; current executive-rule implementation; collection basis and transfer route. Naming Dubai alone does not establish the applicable regime. The government portal confirms the federal framework but does not settle applicability or implementation dates. [Official UAE portal overview](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/digital-uae/data/data-protection-laws.).

### Uzbekistan, later phase

The current LexUZ Article 27-1 is expressly updated by Law ZRU-1125 of 26 March 2026. It mandates domestic storage for listed biometric, genetic and telecommunications-service user data. Other data may be stored/processed abroad through specified adequacy, approved contractual/corporate safeguards or approved international standards. Articles 18, 21 and 30 address processing conditions, demonstrable consent/withdrawal and rights. The Russian text is marked an unofficial translation. [Official legal portal, current consolidated text](https://lex.uz/ru/docs/4396428).

Review needed: confirm the Uzbek authoritative wording, effective amendments, applicable categories and approved adequacy lists/contracts/standards. An older blanket assertion that every Uzbek citizen's analytics requires an Uzbek database is stale. The revision also does not prove that any Railway/TM destination or ordinary processor contract qualifies.

### Georgia, later phase

The current official consolidated statute covers automated processing in Georgia and certain foreign controllers using technical means there. Article 5 sets bases; Article 20 permits withdrawal without explanation and provides termination/erasure within ten working days when no other ground exists. Article 37 governs foreign safeguards and alternative routes, including a permit for a contractual-safeguard route. The current text names the State Audit Office for that permit. [Official Matsne consolidated law, Articles 2, 5, 20, 37](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/5827307).

Review needed: technical-means territorial applicability, lawful basis, representation and destination safeguards/permit. Do not reuse older regulator names or assume every international processor contract is sufficient.

### Germany/EU, later phase

GDPR Article 3 includes offering services or monitoring behavior of people in the EU by an outside controller. Articles 4 and recital 26 distinguish pseudonymization from anonymization. Articles 6–7 require a basis and demonstrable, withdrawable consent where used; Articles 13, 17 and 21 address notice, erasure and objection. Chapter V governs international transfers. [Official GDPR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679).

Germany TDDDG §25 separately requires informed consent for terminal storage/access unless solely necessary for communication or strictly necessary to deliver the expressly requested service. This covers information storage/access, not just personal-data cookies. [Official §25](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/ttdsg/__25.html).

Review needed: persistent analytics ID/storage/access exemption and GDPR basis independently, legitimate-interest assessment if proposed, transfer mechanism/assessment, representative and impact-assessment requirements. First-party status and legitimate interests do not automatically remove §25 consent requirements. Default-on optional longitudinal app tracking in Germany is not established by these sources.

### South Korea, later phase

The official PIPA Article 28-8 text effective 11 September 2026 requires an allowed foreign-transfer route. Separate transfer consent is one route; necessary contract processing/storage with prescribed notice is another. Consent disclosures include destination, recipient, fields, purpose/retention and refusal process/effect. Article 37 covers stopping processing and withdrawing consent, including necessary destruction without delay subject to exceptions. [Current official Article 28-8](https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/lsLinkCommonInfo.do?chrClsCd=010202&lsJoLnkSeq=1029331957), [official Article 37](https://law.go.kr/LSW/lsLawLinkInfo.do?chrClsCd=010202&lsJoLnkSeq=900078593).

Review needed: collection basis under current Article 15, optional versus necessary processing, applicable foreign-controller/representative requirements, transfer disclosures and actual recipients. Do not equate improvement analytics with necessary contractual processing or assume statistical pseudonym exceptions authorize this timeline.

## Google Play requirements, independent of regional basis

Google's User Data policy requires accurate privacy/data-handling disclosures, secure handling and developer responsibility for included SDKs. Where personal/sensitive collection or use is outside reasonable user expectations, prominent in-app disclosure and affirmative consent must precede collection. A privacy policy or Terms alone does not satisfy that requirement. Account-creating apps need an in-app deletion path and an external web deletion resource; associated account data must be addressed and legitimate retained exceptions disclosed. [Official User Data policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10144311?hl=en), [official account-deletion requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13327111?hl=en).

Data Safety includes pseudonymous collection and SDK behavior. Off-device transmission counts as collection. A qualifying processor transfer can fall outside the declaration's definition of sharing, without eliminating collection disclosure. The taxonomy includes app interactions, user/device IDs, crash logs and diagnostics. The form distinguishes required/optional collection and purposes such as analytics. [Official Data Safety instructions](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10787469?hl=en).

Implementation inference: prepare a payload-to-form inventory from actual app/relay/SDK traffic. Do not claim no collection because events use random IDs, pass through AutoTM or are self-hosted. Review build/channel form obligations before submission. A correct form does not establish a lawful regional basis, and a legal consent flow does not establish correct Play declarations.

## Engineering acceptance proposals, not new founder decisions

- Maintain separate purposes and policy checks for product timelines, crash diagnostics, security/abuse logs and core marketplace data. Operational labeling does not exempt identifiable diagnostic payloads.
- Gate collection before creating analytics identifiers, writing queues or emitting events where consent is required. A relay that discards events after the device collects them is insufficient for a prior-consent requirement.
- Give guests a rights path independent of account creation. Do not require identifying a previously pseudonymous guest beyond what the request requires. Define safe proof of control and what can be erased when an installation ID is lost.
- Record consent/basis version and purpose using minimal evidence. Withdrawal stops future collection for that purpose and purges affected queues. Implement deletion of linked raw history, server late-upload rejection and restore deletion replay. Account recovery must not resurrect analytics mappings.
- Carry accepted maximum lifetimes through local queues, ingest, raw stores, diagnostics, backups and exports. Review group/filter combinations for reidentification; a threshold of five does not certify anonymous aggregates.
- Policy/delivery must handle unknown or changed applicability safely. Selected listing city, language, citizenship and device locale are not proof of present physical region. Avoid adding precise-location or fingerprint tracking to solve routing. Define a lawful minimal signal or uniform stricter policy, reviewed before implementation.
- Validate diagnostic SDK metadata and attachments, URLs/query strings, headers, request/response bodies, message text and account association. Security logs need their own minimized allowlist, retention and rights assessment.

## Phase evidence and material choices

Initial Ashgabat invitations require an identified responsible operator/reviewer, documented TM applicability/basis/storage/transfer findings, approved notices and any required consent/withdrawal flow, tested collection/deletion gates, payload evidence and lawful operational-diagnostic treatment. These gates apply only to real pilot collection. Synthetic prototype work and specification development can continue.

Each later region needs a dated readiness record with applicability, basis, terminal-storage rules, rights, destinations/processors, transfer/localization route and actual delivery evidence before enabling its longitudinal analytics. Core-service availability remains separately gated. Telemetry-off does not certify the app's core processing.

The founder may need to choose, once applicability review supplies the facts: an explicit analytics consent/control flow where required; deferred longitudinal analytics with validated operational collection; or a revised compliant topology/pilot scope. The already accepted fallback supports deferral without reopening vendor selection. Default-on cannot be approved universally from the evidence here. Additional regional resources, contracts and paid legal/provisioning work need their own concrete scope and authorization.

Open evidence gaps: legal operator identity/establishment; actual storage, backup and remote-access destinations; authoritative local-language review where translations are used; current Russia post-June-2025 amendments; current UAE implementing rules; current approved Uzbekistan mechanisms; exact app/native-SDK payload inventory; rights proof and phase-routing design. No source fetch established a live provider configuration or a completed legal review.
